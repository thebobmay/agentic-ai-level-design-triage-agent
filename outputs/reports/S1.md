# AI Level Design Triage Report

## Design Brief
I want a beginner friendly first level that still feels a little exciting. Keep the opening approachable for brand new players.

## Interpreted Intent
- Target audience: beginner
- Difficulty target: easy
- Novelty preference: medium
- Desired feel: exciting, approachable
- Hard constraints: (none)
- Soft preferences: beginner friendly, exciting feel, approachable opening
- Detected conflicts: (none)

## Triage Decision
- Action: recommend_revision
- Playtest readiness: revise_before_playtest
- Confidence: high
- Rounds: 1

## Diagnosis
The level is structurally valid and matches the easy difficulty target, but it lacks an opening safe zone for beginners. The first enemy appears at column 3, which is too abrupt for the intended approachable, beginner-friendly experience. The pacing is otherwise balanced, and there are no difficulty spikes. Novelty is within the desired medium range.

## Tradeoff Reasoning
- The absence of an opening safe zone conflicts with the intent for an approachable, beginner-friendly segment. Beginners need a short stretch to orient before encountering hazards.
- The rest of the level is structurally sound, easy, and not derivative, so only the opening needs revision.

## Revision Prescription
- Move the first enemy (E) further right so that the first 5 columns are free of hazards or enemies. (This creates a safe opening stretch, allowing beginners to orient before facing a challenge, which aligns with the approachable and beginner-friendly intent.)

Playtest question: Does the new opening feel welcoming and give beginners enough time to get oriented before the first challenge?

## Independent Critic
- Verdict: approve
The Director's recommendation is sound. The diagnosis correctly identifies that the absence of an opening safe zone is inconsistent with the intent for a beginner-friendly, approachable segment. The prescription to move the first enemy further right to create a safe opening is appropriate and does not overreach. All other aspects of the level are consistent with the brief, and there are no hard constraint violations. The recommendation is well-justified and should be approved for revision before playtest.
