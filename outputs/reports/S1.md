# AI Level Design Triage Report

## Design Brief
I want a beginner friendly first level that still feels a little exciting. Keep the opening approachable for brand new players.

## Interpreted Intent
- Target audience: beginner
- Difficulty target: easy
- Novelty preference: medium
- Desired feel: exciting, approachable
- Hard constraints: (none)
- Soft preferences: beginner friendly, exciting opening, approachable for new players
- Detected conflicts: (none)

## Triage Decision
- Action: recommend_revision
- Playtest readiness: revise_before_playtest
- Confidence: high
- Rounds: 1

## Diagnosis
The level is structurally valid and matches the easy difficulty target, but it lacks an opening safe zone: the first enemy appears at column 3, which is too abrupt for a beginner audience. This conflicts with the intent to be approachable and beginner-friendly. The pacing is otherwise balanced, and novelty is within the desired range.

## Tradeoff Reasoning
- The absence of an opening safe zone is a significant issue for a beginner-friendly level, as new players need a moment to orient before encountering hazards.
- The rest of the level is structurally sound and fits the easy difficulty target, so only the opening needs adjustment.

## Revision Prescription
- Move the first enemy further right so that the first hazard appears no earlier than column 6, creating a short safe stretch at the start. (This gives beginners time to orient and aligns with the intent for an approachable, beginner-friendly segment.)

Playtest question: Does the new opening feel welcoming and give new players enough time to get comfortable before the first challenge?

## Independent Critic
- Verdict: approve
The Director's diagnosis is consistent with the facts and the intent: the absence of an opening safe zone is a clear issue for a beginner-friendly, easy segment, as the first enemy appears at column 3, which is too soon for new players. The recommendation to move the first hazard further right is appropriate and does not overreach. All other aspects (difficulty, novelty, pacing, structural validity) are sound and match the brief. No hard constraints are violated. The recommendation is well-justified and should be sent back for revision as prescribed.
