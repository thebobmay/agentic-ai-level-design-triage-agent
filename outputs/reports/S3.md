# AI Level Design Triage Report

## Design Brief
I want this to be easy and welcoming for brand new players, but also pack in lots of enemies and big gaps so it feels intense and hardcore.

## Interpreted Intent
- Target audience: beginner
- Difficulty target: easy
- Novelty preference: medium
- Desired feel: welcoming, intense, hardcore
- Hard constraints: (none)
- Soft preferences: lots of enemies, big gaps
- Detected conflicts: Beginner accessibility and easy difficulty conflict with the request for lots of enemies, big gaps, and an intense/hardcore feel.

## Triage Decision
- Action: request_clarification
- Playtest readiness: not_ready
- Confidence: high
- Rounds: 1

## Diagnosis
The interpreted intent contains a detected conflict: the brief requests a beginner-friendly, easy segment that is also intense and hardcore, with lots of enemies and big gaps. These requirements are at odds, as intensity, many enemies, and large gaps typically raise difficulty and reduce accessibility for beginners. This ambiguity makes it unclear which aspect should take priority in the design.

## Tradeoff Reasoning
- The candidate is structurally valid and has a safe opening, but the brief's conflicting requirements (easy/beginner vs. intense/hardcore with many enemies and big gaps) make it impossible to confidently judge whether the current difficulty and challenge density are appropriate.
- The candidate features a localized difficulty spike (gap combined with enemy at column 9), which is problematic for a beginner/easy segment but might be acceptable if the 'intense/hardcore' aspect is prioritized. Without clarification, it is unclear which direction to recommend.

## Independent Critic
- Verdict: approve
The Director correctly identified a fundamental conflict in the design brief: the request for a beginner-friendly, easy segment that is also intense, hardcore, and features lots of enemies and big gaps. These requirements are inherently at odds, as intensity and layered hazards (such as a gap combined with an enemy at column 9) typically increase difficulty and reduce accessibility for beginners. The Director's decision to request clarification rather than prescribe edits or approve for playtest is appropriate, as it is not possible to resolve the conflicting intent without further input from the designer. The candidate is structurally valid and has a safe opening, but the ambiguity in the brief prevents a clear judgment on whether the current challenge level is appropriate. No hard constraints are violated, and the Director's reasoning is sound.
