# AI Level Design Triage Report

## Design Brief
I want this to be easy and welcoming for brand new players, but also pack in lots of enemies and big gaps so it feels intense and hardcore.

## Interpreted Intent
- Target audience: beginner
- Difficulty target: easy
- Novelty preference: medium
- Desired feel: welcoming, intense, hardcore
- Hard constraints: (none)
- Soft preferences: lots of enemies, big gaps
- Detected conflicts: easy and welcoming for beginners conflicts with lots of enemies and big gaps for an intense, hardcore feel

## Triage Decision
- Action: request_clarification
- Playtest readiness: not_ready
- Confidence: high
- Rounds: 1

## Diagnosis
The interpreted intent contains a detected conflict: the brief requests an easy, welcoming level for beginners, but also asks for lots of enemies, big gaps, and an intense, hardcore feel. These goals are at odds, as a beginner-friendly level should avoid intensity, large gaps, and high enemy density, while an intense, hardcore level would require these elements and a higher difficulty. The candidate level itself is valid, but the conflicting intent prevents a clear, confident triage decision.

## Tradeoff Reasoning
- The brief's target audience and difficulty ('easy', 'beginner', 'welcoming') conflict with the desire for 'lots of enemies', 'big gaps', and an 'intense, hardcore' feel, which are not suitable for beginners.
- The candidate level is structurally valid and has a safe opening, but contains a localized difficulty spike (gap combined with enemy) that is not appropriate for a true beginner segment.
- Without clarification on which aspect of the intent should take precedence, it is not possible to confidently recommend revision or acceptance.

## Independent Critic
- Verdict: approve
The Director correctly identified a fundamental conflict in the design intent: the brief simultaneously requests an easy, welcoming level for beginners and an intense, hardcore feel with lots of enemies and big gaps. These are mutually exclusive goals for a beginner segment. The candidate level is structurally valid and has a safe opening, but the presence of a difficulty spike (gap combined with enemy) at column 9 is not appropriate for a true beginner level. However, without clarification on which aspect of the intent should take precedence, it is not possible to confidently recommend revision or acceptance. The Director's recommendation to request clarification is sound and respects the hard constraints and the intent as interpreted. No further action is warranted until the designer clarifies their priorities.
