## Design Brief
Reduce the frustration in this level but preserve the core layout. Do not redesign it.

- Difficulty target: medium
- Novelty preference: medium
- Desired feel: less frustrating

## Triage Decision

## Diagnosis
The candidate is structurally valid and matches the medium difficulty target, with a high novelty score and a balanced pacing profile. However, there is a localized difficulty spike at column 9 where a gap is combined with an enemy, which can create a moment of frustration. This conflicts with the intent to reduce frustration and provide a less frustrating experience for a general audience. The rest of the level is sound, and the opening safe zone is appropriate.

## Tradeoff Reasoning
- The level is structurally valid and does not require redesign, respecting the hard constraints.
- Difficulty and pacing are appropriate for a medium segment, and novelty is high, which is positive for the brief.
- However, the detected spike (gap plus enemy) at column 9 is likely to frustrate players, conflicting with the explicit intent to reduce frustration.

## Revision Prescription
- Separate the enemy from the gap at column 9 by moving the enemy at least 2 columns away from the gap, or relocating the gap so it does not coincide with the enemy. (This edit will reduce the frustration caused by the layered hazard, aligning the segment with the intent for a less frustrating, medium-difficulty experience while preserving the core layout.)

Playtest question: After revising, does the segment still feel fair and less frustrating at the former spike location, while maintaining medium difficulty overall?

## Independent Critic
- Verdict: approve
The Director's recommendation is sound. The diagnosis correctly identifies that, while the level is structurally valid and matches the medium difficulty and novelty targets, the localized spike where a gap is combined with an enemy could create frustration, which conflicts with the explicit intent to reduce frustration for a general audience. The prescribed revision—separating the enemy from the gap—directly addresses this issue without violating the hard constraints (preserving the core layout and not redesigning the level). No other issues are present, and the rest of the level is appropriate for the brief. The recommendation does not overreach or underreach.
